FOR THE POSSE

How to Play Online

Everything you need on your phone at the table

Before you start

  1. Tell the site who you are. The first time you open it, it asks “Who are you playing?” — pick your character. You can also tap ☆ This is me on your sheet. Do this on every phone or computer you use. That’s how you get your turn alerts, the Warden’s roll requests and the right name on your dice.
  2. Find your sheet under Posse Sheets. It opens in view mode: Health, Grit, Statuses, ammo and money stay live. On a phone it starts in Table view — just what you need mid-game; tap Full sheet for the rest. Tap Edit to change anything else. Want your own face on it? Tap Add a photo in the sheet header, frame the headshot, save — it shows on your token too.

Take the tour of your sheet A one-minute walk through your character sheet and the buttons you’ll use most.

Any time

  • Roll dice — the button in the bottom corner of every page. Pick Black and Gold dice, say what it’s for, roll. Everyone sees it in the Table Log.
  • The Warden asks for a roll — a pop-up appears on whatever page you’re on. Tap Roll. If someone else is rolling, you get a Want to help? pop-up — tap Help to roll half your dice and add to theirs.
  • Sound — dice, cards, gunshots, Forstalls and pop-ups make noise. Tap the speaker in the menu bar to mute (on phones it’s in Menu, with a volume slider).
  • Table Log — every roll, hit and award, newest first. The number on the button is what you haven’t seen yet.
  • Forstall memory slots — once the posse decodes a monster on the Forstall Scanner, pick it in one of your Forstall’s four memory slots (on your sheet or the Battle Map). Only programmed monsters feel the +1 from your Sweep.
  • Pick a lock — when the Warden sends you a lock it opens on your phone. Roll Finesse (each Hit = one peek at the next card’s color), then call each card Higher or Lower. A tie breaks the pick; set the pins in a row to open it.
  • Posse Stash — the posse’s shared money and gear (World → Posse Stash, or the button on your sheet’s Inventory). Anyone can put things in or take them out; every move shows up in the Table Log. A spare gun keeps its upgrades and a spare horse its Bond while it’s in there.
  • Sheet sections — on a character sheet the side menu (a strip across the top on a phone) lists the posse (tap to switch; yours is starred) and the sheet’s sections: Fight, Skills & Prestige, Gear & Rides, Story & Titles, or the Whole sheet. Print always prints the whole sheet.
  • At a glance — the top of your finished sheet sums up what you can do right now: your Abilities and Aces-in-the-Hole with their Grit and uses left (tap Use), any Statuses on you with what they do and the Skill that shakes them (tap Relieve), and your weapons’ dice at each range, Grit, ammo and upgrades.
  • Contests — when the Warden opens a horse race or a trick-shot contest you get an invite. Enter (the fees make the winner’s pot), and put a side bet on anyone. A race is three legs (Finesse, Nerve, then your pick), and your horse adds dice by breed and Bond; no horse, and you rent one. Trick shots follow Dobytown’s rules (Iron Road p. 109). When the Warden starts it, everyone watches it play out.
  • Boot Hill — when a character dies they get a headstone (World → Boot Hill): how they went down, the day it happened, and what they’re remembered for from the Table Log. Write their epitaph there.
  • Records — Journal → Records keeps everyone’s lifetime winnings and losses at the card tables, the drinking contest, the carnival and the contests, with a champion for each game.
  • Search — the Search button (bottom right) lets you search a downed enemy, a wagon or mech, rubble, or a monster’s lair. You roll Intuition: 1–2 Hits turn up pocket money or Scrap, 3–4 a Basic item, 5 or more a Premium one, and with 7 or more there’s a small chance of Elite. Monster bodies give sellable parts; lairs hold what their victims left behind. The Warden OKs the find, then it’s in your Inventory. (Salvaging a wreck for Scrap is the Warden’s call, p. 93.)
  • Trade — the Trade button (bottom right, or on your sheet’s Inventory) lets you offer money and anything on your sheet (guns, gear, your horse or mech) to another player, and ask for something of theirs back. They get a pop-up to take the deal or say no. Things move sheet and all: a gun keeps its upgrades, a horse keeps its Bond.
  • Whisper — the button by Roll dice sends the Warden a secret message only they see. The Warden can answer (or whisper to you first) with a pop-up.
  • Backpack — when the Warden hands you an item or a secret note it pops up on your phone. Tap Show the posse to share it; everything shared lives in the Backpack (World menu).
  • Journal — the posse’s quests (with the steps you’ve worked out), a corkboard of clues, and every newspaper you’ve made the front page of. Add your own notes to any quest or clue; the whole posse sees them. New quests, leads and papers pop up on your screen when the Warden reveals them.
  • Card games — when the Warden opens a table you get an invite, and it’s your real wallet money. Poker is five-card draw. Faro is betting against the bank: put money on any card, and it wins when that rank comes up as the player’s card and loses as the bank’s card (copper a bet to flip it). Keep an eye on the dealer, too. Liar’s Dice: everyone puts in, rolls five dice under a cup and bids on how many of a face are on the whole table (ones are wild); raise the bid or call “Liar!” — whoever’s wrong loses a die, and the last one holding dice takes the pot. Blackjack: bet, then get closer to 21 than the dealer without going over — hit, stand, or double down on your first two cards; a blackjack pays 3 to 2. You can count the cards (Intuition) to learn whether the next card is high or low, or use a shiner (Finesse) to see the dealer’s hole card — get caught and your bet is gone. Drinking contest: everyone pays in and takes a shot each round — roll Nerve and match the round number in Hits (1, then 2, then 3…). You can spend up to 3 Grit per shot for extra dice (it stays spent till you rest). A miss makes you Drunk (fewer dice) and costs 1 Health; three Drunk and you pass out and wake up Dazed. Last one standing takes the pot; the first one out pays the bar tab. Try the spittoon trick (Finesse) to skip a shot, or needle an NPC (Charm) to shake them. At Liar’s Dice you can peek under an NPC’s cup (Intuition) or stare them down so they can’t call you a liar (Charm). At poker, on your turn you can also try a Skill move: read an NPC’s tell (Intuition), bluff (Charm), or palm a card during the draw (Finesse; get caught and your hand is thrown in). Tap Rules & key at the table for how each game works and what every Skill move rolls against. Step away with × and come back with the chip in the corner.
  • Wanted — the posting board for each town, starting with wherever the posse is on the Map. Tap a poster to see it up close, and tap Take the bounty to go after them: it goes in the Journal as a quest for the whole posse and ticks itself off as you bring them in. The Warden pays out bounties straight into your wallet.
  • Map — tap a town to read about it and drag your token to show where you are. Crowded spot? Type in Find a place above the panel, or zoom right in. The East Portal button at the top left opens the town map: tap a lettered building (or pick it from the list) for what’s there, who you’ll meet, and a link to its prices in the Store.
  • The carnival — when the Warden brings the Traveling Carnival to town you get an invite. Buy a $0.25 ticket and play the booths with your own dice: Horseshoe Toss (Finesse), Wheel of Fortune (bet $0.05 to $1.00), Archery (3B a shot against a carnie), the High Striker (Nerve, 5 Hits rings the bell), the fortune teller, the pie eating contest (Nerve) and the Greased Pig Chase (Finesse, 3 Hits holds on). Each booth rolls your dice (with the Skill it uses) and then plays out on screen. Wins give prize vouchers; trade them at the prize booth for a prize in your Inventory. The chip in the corner takes you back. The carnival and the saloon tables have their own background sounds; the Warden can also play sounds and background loops (a train, a river) for everyone. Turn background sounds off in Settings (the cog) if you’d rather not.
  • NPCs — everyone the posse has met. Jot down what you learn; the whole posse sees your notes. Something at the table crossed a line? The Whisper box has a Sheriff’s Badge: the Warden cuts away from the scene and nobody is told it was you. Got a score to settle? Challenge to a Duel on their card asks the Warden, who accepts or says no. A Duel pops up for the whole posse to watch. Posse Standing at the top shows how each faction sees the posse as a whole (Revered +2B to Helpful +1B, down to Hostile −2B Charm when you deal with them); the Warden sets it, and a change shows up in the Table Log.
  • Store — pick who’s shopping and ask to buy or sell. The Warden okays it and the money moves on your sheet.
  • Look up a rule — the ? at the top right (or Menu → Look up a rule on a phone). Type a word like “dodge”, “cover” or “bleeding” to get the rule in plain words with its Guidebook page. The more link next to each Status on your sheet opens the same thing.

Keyboard shortcuts

On a laptop: R roll dice · L Table Log · / look up a rule · B Battle Map · ? the full list. They don’t fire while you’re typing.

In a fight

  1. The Warden starts combat. Everyone’s turn-order roll pops up, and the order sits in a strip in the bottom-left corner (tap it to hide or show).
  2. When it’s your turn a red bar drops in at the top of the page, the browser tab flashes “Your turn!” (even if you’re on another tab) and phones buzz. Tap Go to Battle Map. (Or End my turn if you’re passing.)
  3. On the Battle Map, your character’s card opens beside your token when it’s your turn. Pick an action there: for Attack, the enemies you can hit light up, so click one on the map (or in the card), pick a weapon, and press Roll. Quick moves in the sidebar are one tap: “Shoot the Wolf” with your best weapon for that range, Grit cost shown. Forstall: on your turn the Forstall tile lets you Scan a monster on the board (3 Grit, roll Intuition, then one guess at its frequency) or Sweep. Standing next to a Forstall the Warden put on the map (within 1″)? You can work that one too — one person a round. Press and hold any spot on the map (or right-click) to ping it — everyone sees a marker with your name for a few seconds. The turn panel also shows your Grit and everything else you can do:
    • Attack — pick a target; the map works out the range.
    • Move — drag your token; it costs Grit, shown as you drag.
    • Dodge — spend Grit to soak the next hit.
    • Ability, Use Item, Relieve a Status, Improvise.
    • Prepare — pay now and hold an action for a trigger (“when the bandit moves…”). When it’s set off, a pop-up lets you fire it from any page.
    • Fool’s Grit — 1 Health for 1 more Grit.
    • Forstall — if you carry one, Sweep: spend its Grit and a battery charge and roll its dice. Every monster inside the shaded Range loses that much Grit when its turn starts or it walks in, +1 for frequencies programmed into your memory slots. With a Crystal Burst Fuse and a crystal, Burst drives a programmed monster off. Keep clear of other Sweeping Forstalls (Edison’s Rule 1).
    Made a mistake? Undo steps back one action, or restarts your turn.
  4. Tap End my turn when you’re done. The next player gets their bar.
  5. Hit 0 Health? Your sheet shows the Bleeding Out rolls — make them there.
